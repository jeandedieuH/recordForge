# RecordForge 2.4.0

## Highlights

- **Clip speed control.** Right-click a video, camera, or audio clip and
  choose **Speed**, or use the new Playback speed section in the inspector,
  to retime it between 0.1× and 16×. Clips that share a span — screen,
  camera, and mic recorded together — retime as one, grouped clips follow
  their group, and the change ripples through the rest of the timeline so
  later clips stay in sync.
- **ProRes export.** A new ProRes format writes mastering-grade 10-bit
  4:2:2 movies (.mov) with uncompressed PCM audio. Pick Proxy, LT,
  Standard, or HQ to match your finishing workflow in Final Cut Pro,
  Premiere, or DaVinci Resolve. ProRes is a Pro feature.
- **Frame borders.** Draw a rim inside the frame from the Layout panel —
  solid, gradient, double, or glass styles with adjustable thickness,
  colour, and opacity. Camera bubbles get the same four border styles in
  the clip inspector.

## Improvements

- **Explicit output format.** Export now has Resolution (project canvas,
  720p, 1080p — 1440p and 2160p on Pro) and Frame rate (project, 24, 30, or
  60 fps) controls, independent of the preset. Tiers only ever scale down,
  never upscale, and presets now just prefill the two fields.
- **Honest exports.** Finished export jobs now report any effect that was
  rendered below full quality — for example motion blur or a glass loupe
  on the compatibility renderer — as a "Rendered with simplifications"
  notice in the export view, instead of silently differing from the editor
  preview.

## Fixes

- **Frame rates are honoured.** The 60 fps presets no longer force the
  encoder to 60 fps on slower compositions. Exports now compose every frame
  at the requested rate instead of duplicating frames to fill the gap.

## Known issues

- ProRes always uses the software ProRes encoder (no hardware path yet),
  and the files are large by design — use Proxy or LT for offline edits.
- Editing a project in an older RecordForge version strips the new export
  fields (resolution, frame rate, ProRes profile) and border settings when
  it re-saves the project.

## Internal

- ADR 020 establishes GPU-first effects: new pixel effects target the wgpu
  compositor, the compatibility renderer keeps fast documented
  approximations, and every downgrade is persisted on the job as
  `EffectDowngrade { effect, reason }` (`outputs.renderNotes`).
- Clip retiming is a pure timeline command (`set-clip-speed`) backed by
  `time-warp.ts`, which remaps later clips through the changed span.
- ProRes renders through `prores_ks` on both renderers and chunks like
  other video exports via MOV intermediates; its disk preflight uses a
  per-profile bits-per-pixel table.
- Canvas borders render as tiny-skia plates on the compatibility path and
  an analytic SDF ring in the GPU composite pass. A mid-timeline
  side-by-side camera layout that moves the screen rect cannot reuse the
  static plate, so the compatibility renderer drops the canvas border and
  reports a `canvas-border` downgrade; the GPU renderer is exact.
