# RecordForge 2.3.1

## Highlights

- **Smart Cut projects export reliably.** Timelines produced by Smart Cut —
  which can split one clip into hundreds of pieces — no longer stall or hang
  during export. The audio mix, camera overlay decode, and video segment
  pipeline all scale linearly with the number of pieces on both the
  compatibility and GPU renderers.
- **Seamless playback across cuts.** The editor preview now uses a pair of
  pre-rolled media slots per source instead of one element per clip, so
  playing through a densely cut timeline no longer stutters or drops audio
  at every splice point.

## Improvements

- Smart Cut detection now merges cuts that would leave a kept island shorter
  than 200 ms — those read as clicks — and snaps cut edges to the canvas
  frame grid so every spliced piece covers a whole number of frames.
- Exported video now stays frame-exact against audio on multi-segment
  timelines. Previously, off-grid cuts accumulated up to a frame of drift
  per splice, which could leave video over a second ahead of audio on a
  heavily cut project.
- Camera overlays are decoded as one stream per camera track instead of one
  process per clip piece, dramatically cutting the number of FFmpeg
  processes spawned for cut-heavy exports.
- Splitting an audio clip (via Smart Cut, range delete, or split) now
  divides its fade-in/fade-out and volume keyframes correctly between the
  pieces, instead of copying the full envelope onto every piece.

## Fixes

- Fixed exports getting stuck during "compositing timeline tracks" on
  Smart-Cut timelines — the audio filter graph was quadratic in the number
  of segments and could run for minutes without finishing.
- Fixed audio dropouts and 100–300 ms video stalls during preview playback
  at each cut boundary; the standby slot is pre-seeked and swapped in at
  the splice.
- Fixed the preview mounting one media element per clip piece — a 206-piece
  Smart Cut could leave 600+ elements preloading at once.

## Known issues

- Exports of single-segment or on-grid timelines are unaffected by the
  frame-snapping change; only multi-segment plans may produce slightly
  different frame counts (by design).
